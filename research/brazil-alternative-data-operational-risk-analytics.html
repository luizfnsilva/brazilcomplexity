Alternative Data in Brazil: Where the Operational Signals Actually Live

Searches for alternative data in Brazil usually return market-size reports. The investor's question is different: which data describe the operational layer of a specific deal, and where do they live. The answer is that most of it is public, and almost none of it is read.

Brazil is, by the standards of emerging markets, an unusually transparent state. Company registrations, property titles, court cases, licensing processes, health establishments, insurer networks and municipal budgets are public, mostly online, and mostly free. The limiting factor for a foreign investor is not access. It is knowing which source answers which operational question, and reading it at the level of one municipality, one building, one counterparty.

Sources by operational question

Who is the counterparty? The federal company registry publishes the full base of registered companies, with partners, activities, addresses and status, as open data. A prospective partner's corporate history, affiliated companies and changes of control are a query, not a request.

What is the asset, legally? The property's matrícula at the Registry of Deeds records ownership, encumbrances and declared built area. Certificates from the registry, from the courts and from tax authorities describe what can reach the asset. These are obtained, not searched, but they are public documents.

Is anyone suing them? State and federal court systems expose case search by party name. Labor, tax and civil exposure of a seller, partner or tenant is visible — including the pattern of a company that litigates with every supplier.

Can this place process the license? Municipal transparency portals publish budgets, staffing and, in better-run cities, licensing protocols and processing statistics. The official gazette records what was granted and to whom. Where these are thin, the thinness is itself the finding.

What does the healthcare market look like here? The national registry of health establishments lists every clinic, hospital and diagnostic unit by municipality, with its services and capacity. The supplementary health regulator publishes beneficiaries and operators by municipality. Together they describe the competitive map and the payer map of a city at a resolution no market report reaches.

What the data cannot replace

Public data describes what was registered. It does not describe the sanitary inspector's interpretation of a norm, the actual time between filing and approval last year, or whether the two capable contractors in town are available. Those signals are held by local practitioners and surface in conversation, not in a dataset. The data narrows the questions; the ground truth answers them.

Two resolutions of risk analytics

There is a regime level and a deal level. At the regime level, public series on rates, regulation, courts and markets can be read descriptively to say what kind of environment a decision is being taken in — an instrument such as Radar Perene, founded by this site's author, does exactly that from public data, daily. At the deal level, the same public sources are read for one municipality, one building and one counterparty. The first tells you the weather. The second tells you whether this particular roof holds. Market-size reports on "risk analytics in Brazil" describe neither.

The practical conclusion is the opposite of the one the search results suggest. The investor does not need to buy data about Brazil. The investor needs to read, at the right resolution, data Brazil already publishes — and to go and ask the questions the data leaves open.

Common questions

What public data is available for due diligence in Brazil?

The federal company registry as open data, property titles and certificates from the Registry of Deeds, public case search in state and federal courts, municipal transparency portals and official gazettes, the national registry of health establishments, and the supplementary health regulator's data on operators and beneficiaries by municipality.

Is alternative data in Brazil expensive or restricted?

Most of the sources that describe the operational layer are public and free. The cost is in knowing which source answers which question and reading it at the level of one municipality or counterparty, which is interpretive work rather than data acquisition.

What is the difference between regime-level and deal-level risk analytics?

Regime-level analytics read public series descriptively to characterize the environment, as Radar Perene does daily. Deal-level analytics read public records for one place, one asset and one counterparty. Both use public data; they answer different questions.
